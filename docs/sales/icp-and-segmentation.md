# Ideal Customer Profile & Segmentation

Last updated: Oct 21, 2026

## Who we're looking for

**The ideal merchant** is an owner-operated independent on a walkable BIA strip, with customers who come back at least monthly, where the owner (or a manager who can say yes) is in the shop most days, and who either has no loyalty program or one they're mildly annoyed by (paper cards, a paid app, a fee).

**Hard filters (skip or park as `lost` with a reason):**

- No storefront, or not on/near the target strip.
- Corporate-owned chain store where the local manager can't sign (e.g. a national pharmacy chain store). Franchisees are different; see [playbooks/franchises-multi-location.md](playbooks/franchises-multi-location.md).
- Business where earning would be illegal or tasteless (cannabis retail, payday lenders, vape-only shops). Cannabis promotion is tightly restricted in Ontario; don't sign cannabis stores.
- The owner says no twice. Log it, be nice, move on. Revisit after launch week when the street is buzzing.

## How to score a vertical

Each vertical is scored 1–5 on seven factors. The weights reflect phase 1: **we need a large, active network first, then revenue later.**

| Factor | Weight | 5 means | 1 means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repeat frequency | 25% | Daily or weekly visits | Once a year or less |
| Network value | 20% | Drives foot traffic that other shops on the street benefit from | Customers come and go, never browse the strip |
| Ease of yes | 15% | Owner is on site, decides alone, no head office | Committees, head office, landlord, banner rules |
| Current-method pain | 10% | Paper cards, nothing at all, or paying fees for a tool they barely use | Happy with a tool built into their POS |
| Ticket size | 10% | $50+ average | Under $5 |
| Likelihood to pay for Plus/Pro later | 15% | Clear, painful workflow the add-on fixes (approvals, order-ahead) | No obvious paid need |
| Activation speed | 5% | Can get 25 members in a week | Will take months |

**Score = sum(score × weight).** Max is 5.0.

## Launch order (and why)

We sign in **waves** on every strip. The order matters more than the total.

### Wave 1: Traffic anchors (days 1–5)
**Cafés, bakeries, grocery/convenience.**
These shops see the same people daily or weekly. They hit 25 active members in days, not weeks. They are what make a customer's wallet pass feel useful on day one. A strip without a busy café on board is not a network.

### Wave 2: Frequent personal services (days 3–10)
**Barbers, salons/spas, fitness studios, pharmacies (front store).**
Regular cadence (every 2–6 weeks), owners on site, and strong word-of-mouth. Pharmacies add credibility with older residents but can be slower to sign (banner rules, owner-pharmacist time). Start the conversation in wave 1 so the yes lands in wave 2.

### Wave 3: Destination & browse retail (days 5–14)
**Restaurants, bars/pubs, retail boutiques, bookstores/gift, florists, pet stores.**
Lower frequency but high **network value**: these are where customers discover each other's shops. They benefit most from the traffic waves 1 and 2 create. Pitch them with the live count: "14 shops on the street already, 600 members."

### Wave 4 (from day 1, in parallel): Future payers
**Phone/computer repair, auto shops, alterations/dry cleaning, print shops.**
Repair and alterations score well overall only because they are the likeliest to pay later; they add little daily traffic and activate slowly. Auto and print score lowest on traffic. Sign them free now and **flag them** in the rep console notes as "Pro candidate." They are the founder's first calls when Pro opens. The PRD's own test: ask 5 repair or auto shops for a $100 refundable Pro pilot deposit. Do that alongside their free signup, run by the founder, not reps.

### Franchises / multi-location: founder-led, any time
Unlimited free locations is our sharpest edge against per-location pricing. One yes can add 3–10 locations. Founder handles these; reps pass them up.

## Segment notes you'll actually use

| If you see… | It usually means… | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper punch cards on the counter | Owner already believes in loyalty | Easiest yes. Offer to honour their old cards (see playbooks) |
| A SoPoints sticker or card | They're paying 5% on the free plan (re-verify) or a monthly fee | Use [scripts/sopoints-switch.md](scripts/sopoints-switch.md) |
| A Square terminal | Possibly paying for Square Loyalty | Ask; use [scripts/square-loyalty-users.md](scripts/square-loyalty-users.md) |
| A QR code for a wallet stamp card (Loopy, Stamp Me, etc.) | Owner likes no-download | "Same no-download join, plus the whole street" |
| Nothing at all | Never thought about it, or tried and gave up | Lead with "we do it for you, 20 minutes" |
| A line out the door | High-volume anchor | Come back at a quiet hour. Never pitch during a rush |
| Owner says "I'm part of a banner" (pharmacy, hardware, grocery) | There may be a banner loyalty contract | Ask them to check their agreement before signing; log "banner check" |

## Lead fields to fill in the rep console

Every lead should have: business name, vertical (use the product's vertical list: `cafe`, `restaurant`, `bakery`, `bar`, `grocery`, `pharmacy`, `retail`, `bookstore`, `florist`, `pet`, `salon`, `barber`, `spa`, `fitness`, `repair_electronics`, `repair_auto`, `alterations`, `dry_cleaning`, `print_shop`, `other`), address, contact name and role, phone, email (only if published or given to you), **current loyalty method** (free text, e.g. "paper punch card", "SoPoints", "Square Loyalty"), POS seen at the counter, best visit time, and next action date.
