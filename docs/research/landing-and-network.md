## 1. Who this is for

Three buyers land on one site. The current site speaks to one of them.

| Buyer | Who they are | What they use today | What they pay today | Where they are in the GTA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Independent shop owner** (café, bakery, salon, repair, retail) | 1 to 3 locations, owner is often on the counter, reads on a phone between customers | Paper stamp cards, Square or Clover POS, Instagram, sometimes a loyalty app | Square Loyalty from CA$60 per location per month (W), SoPoints Free costs 5% of sales through the program (W), FiveStars about $269 to $349 a month (S, sources conflict) | 85-ish BIAs; the City counts 84 established (S, 2025 staff report) |
| **Restaurant owner**, South Asian first (restaurants, sweet shops, tiffin kitchens, halal grocers with a kitchen) | Often family-run, staff read Urdu, Hindi or Punjabi, WhatsApp is the main customer channel, catering and festival rushes carry the year | DoorDash and Uber Eats for discovery, Toast or Square POS, spreadsheets and WhatsApp for tiffin | DoorDash Canada: pickup 8% (Plus, Premier) or 10% (Basic), delivery up to 29% on Premier (W). ChowNow commission-free tiers $119 / $199 / $298 plus 2.95% + $0.29 (S, third-party, may be dated) | Ridgeway Plaza (Mississauga), Gerrard India Bazaar (Toronto, a BIA that exists; about 125 businesses per an undated article, treat as approximate) (S), Brampton |
| **BIA executive director** | 1 to 4 staff, answers to a volunteer board of members and to City Council, runs events and a newsletter | Spreadsheets, foot-traffic counters, social reach, event headcount, occasionally a passport or gift-card program | The levy funds the operating budget; the City collects it on commercial property tax bills and Council approves the budget (S). Grants top it up (below) | 84 BIAs, TABIA is the umbrella; Digital Main Street started inside TABIA in 2016 (S) |

### How BIAs fund and report (research)

- **Levy.** A special charge on commercial and industrial properties inside the BIA, shown as its own line on the tax bill. Members approve the budget at the annual general meeting; City Council approves it before the City collects. In the 2020 cycle 24 BIAs had operating budgets of $10,013,513 raised through a levy of $6,490,804, so about a third came from grants, surplus, sponsors and festival revenue (S, [City staff report 2020](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2020/ec/bgrd/backgroundfile-146432.pdf), 84 BIAs per the [2025 report](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2025/cc/bgrd/backgroundfile-252683.pdf)). Capital work has a 50/50 City cost-share option (S).
- **Grants.**
  - [Main Street Innovation Fund](https://www.toronto.ca/business-economy/business-operation-growth/business-incentives/main-street-innovation-fund) (W): up to $25,000 per eligible BIA or not-for-profit for activations, placemaking and technology. The round open today is limited to Little Jamaica BIAs (Eglinton Hill, Fairbank Village, Little Jamaica, Oakwood Village), projects run May 3 to Sept 10, 2027, deadline **Oct 26, 2026**, audited financials required, a success-measurement plan is part of the application and final reporting has no extensions. An earlier search summary said $25,000 or $50,000; the fund page says $25,000.
  - Digital Main Street, started by TABIA with the City in 2016, offers a Digital Transformation Grant of up to $2,500 to individual businesses via OBIAA and TABIA (S, [Toronto news release](https://www.toronto.ca/news/federal-and-provincial-governments-partner-to-expand-city-of-torontos-digital-main-street-program-across-ontario-to-help-more-local-businesses-during-covid-19-pandemic/)). That is a merchant grant, not a BIA one.
- **What they report to a board.** The OBIAA / TABIA return-on-investment study (W, [McMaster-hosted report, 2024](https://research.degroote.mcmaster.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/98/2024/07/Perception-and-Action-in-a-Shifting-Retail-World-roi-of-bias-report.pdf), Appendix A) narrowed 200+ candidate measures to 30 indicators and seven key ones, including actual event attendance, conversion rate and average dwell time. Two lines matter to us:
  - **Conversion rate**, defined as "event / promotion visitors converted to regular BIA visitors", had **no existing data source** (confidence 0 on every scale, no reporting communities). The study said consistent tracking was beyond the capacity of OBIAA, TABIA and members, and listed loyalty cards and apps that share analytics with the BIA as options, with two cautions: loyalty programs are seen as risky and costly because redemption risk is high, and a BIA-owned program might compete with its own members. Electronic gift cards were costly because they charge retailers 8%.
  - Only 38% of BIAs collect customer-experience data and 19% collect it extensively, while 52% say it is critical.
- **Shop-local campaign results.** We found no published sales or participation results for a Toronto passport campaign. The Oct 2025 CityPlace and Fort York BIA digital passport (a sponsored [blogTO post](https://www.blogto.com/sponsored/2025/10/cityplace-fort-york-bia-digital-passport/), page returned 403 so only the search snippet was read) reports no outcome; the City's March 2025 "Love Local" campaign was a citywide tariff response with no measured impact; the Bloor passport ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/bloor-passport-1.4170871), date unclear) lists 70+ businesses and no sales. **Implication:** nobody in this market publishes results. A BIA board report with real before/after numbers is both our product and our best marketing asset once the first campaign runs.

| Chowbus (S) | Not found with Canadian pricing; Capterra reviewer calls the monthly fee high for an SMB | Do not cite a number |

### What converts SMB owners (and how sure we are)

1. **Proof they can check.** Square and Owner.com lead with numbers and named owners. We have no customers yet, so the page shows *live network counts* (hidden until true) and a *sample* report clearly labelled, and reserves a quote slot for a real owner. We never show invented testimonials or logos.
2. **Price clarity.** 59% of SMB SaaS buyers say they would not have bought without a free trial (S, [Localogy 2023 survey](https://www.localogy.com/2023/12/how-important-are-free-trials-in-smb-saas), self-reported). Freemium is the top SMB model at 29% (same source). Vendor-blog benchmarks put freemium-to-paid at 2.6 to 3.7% against 14 to 25% for trials, but those are weak, conflicting sources; our own pricing doc assumes 35% conversion and says that is the main thing to validate on pilot shops. Design response: keep Free as the trust anchor, show real prices for everything else, and say honestly who has paid features today.
3. **Time-to-value.** SoPoints sells "this afternoon", Owner.com "about a week". We have a stronger claim, a rep who sets it up in 20 minutes, so the landing page gives three dated steps ending at day 30 with a target of 25 active members (from the PRD).
4. **"Free forever" trust.** SoPoints's free plan has a 5% fee and no active rewards (W), which is what an owner will have read already. Our table names it, with the date, and says what is and is not free. Pricing v2 moved promos, email, SMS and POS auto-award to paid plans, so the old FAQ line "they're coming soon" is now wrong for restaurants (the restaurant pack is GA).
5. **Segment first.** Three audiences, one page: the current build makes restaurants scroll to the bottom of `/pricing` to find themselves. A router row and a dedicated restaurant page fix that.

## 3. Jobs to be done

Ranked by frequency × pain.

**Shop owner (visiting the landing page)**
1. Know the real cost and whether "free" has a catch.
2. Be live without giving up counter time.
3. See that it brings regulars back and that others on the street use it.
4. Own my customer data and be able to leave.
5. Know whether it works with my POS and what that costs.

**Restaurant owner**
1. Stop losing 8 to 29% of an order to a delivery app, and know the real number before switching.
2. Fill the festival and Ramadan rush: pre-orders for degh pots and sweet boxes, catering quotes with deposits.
3. Run tiffin subscribers (skips, pauses, routes, recurring payment) without WhatsApp and spreadsheets.
4. Talk to customers on WhatsApp, and let cooks and counter staff read screens in Urdu, Hindi or Punjabi.
5. Show halal and dietary labels, and collect reviews.
6. Not be left to do the typing: someone digitizes the menu and moves the tiffin list.

**BIA executive director**
1. Prove to the board that an event moved real visits, especially the "event visitors who became regular visitors" the province's own study could not measure.
2. Justify the spend (levy line or a grant) with a budget and measurement plan.
3. Get shops to take part without adding work for BIA staff.
4. Run a prize draw without breaking contest law.
5. Know whether the street has enough shops and shoppers on the network before booking.
6. Hand the board a one-page report at the next meeting.

## 5. What we deliberately don't build

- **No invented proof.** No fake logos, testimonials, counts or "trusted by". Live counts are hidden until the network has more than 10 shops. The sample report is always labelled "Sample data".
- **No POS logo wall** until at least one integration is live and available on the plan the visitor is on.
- **No claim that restaurants save $750 to $1,500 on $5,000.** Only the sourced pickup/delivery split.
- **No "we deliver".** The platform has no drivers; copy says pickup or your own delivery.
- **No halal certification claim.** Owners label dishes; we show and filter.
- **No ROI calculator for loyalty.** We have no data to support lift for a shop; Square's lift numbers are not ours to cite.
- **No customer list for BIAs.** Aggregates only; shoppers consented to a shop, not to the BIA.
- **No sales-amount reporting for BIAs in v1.** Visits and new members only. Sales needs a shop opt-in and POS data; the page says "planned".
- **No self-serve BIA checkout.** Campaigns are invoiced and approved by an admin, as today.
- **No gated PDFs.** The sample report and the grant one-pager download without an email.
- **No live-chat widget, no annual/monthly toggle on shop plans** (annual is a single line under the price).
- **No fully translated marketing site in v1** (see build spec; the restaurant page ships English with scripts in the examples and a language-aware contact form).

**Known limits of the mockups.**
- Placeholder tokens such as `{live}`, `{n}` and `{corridor}` mark where live data goes.
- The Figma environment had no Gurmukhi font, so Punjabi script rendered blank and the mock shows "Punjabi" in Latin. Devanagari and Urdu render. In code load Noto Sans Devanagari, Noto Sans Gurmukhi and Noto Nastaliq Urdu (or Noto Sans Arabic) for the matching locales.
- The pricing calculator slider is drawn static ($5,000).
- Phone mocks are schematic; use the real `PhoneFrame` mocks in code.

### 7.2 New and changed endpoints and fields

All under `/v1`, contracts in `packages/shared/src/api/`. Docs go in `docs/engineering/api/public.md`, `campaigns.md`, `network.md`.

1. **`GET /v1/public/plans`** (exists): extend `network` with `members: number`, `multiShopMemberPct: number`, `liveStreets: number`. The web hides `LiveProof` when `activeMerchants < 10`.
2. **`GET /v1/public/restaurants/founding`** → `{ corridors: [{ slug, name, taken, total: 20 }] }`, from `merchants.foundingOffer`. Used by `FoundingStrip` and the pricing founding line. Cache 300 s.
3. **`GET /v1/neighbourhoods/:id/overview?from&to`** (neighbourhood manager or admin, same checks as `GET /v1/admin/neighbourhoods/:id/merchants`):
   ```
   { period:{from,to}, prior:{from,to},
     shops:{ live, members, joinedInPeriod },
     activeMembers:{ value, delta }, multiShopPct:{ value, deltaPoints },
     visits:{ value, deltaPct },
     weekly:[{ weekStart, visits, campaignIds:[uuid] }],
     shopsTable:[{ id, name, status, activated, visits, newMembers }],
     gates:{ liveMonths, activeShops, activeCustomers, multiShopPct,
             thresholds:{ liveMonths:6, activeShops:20, activeCustomers:1500, multiShopPct:25 } } }
   ```
   `members` for "of 74" needs the BIA's member count: add `neighbourhoods.biaMemberCount` (nullable int, admin-editable). Thresholds come from one shared constant (the proposed `unlockPerNeighbourhood` in `network-monetization.md`), also enforced by `selectSponsored` and campaign creation later. Aggregates only.
4. **`GET /v1/neighbourhoods/:id/report?from&to&format=json|csv`** returns the period report (campaigns inside the period, their `CampaignReport` summaries, overview deltas). PDF is `window.print()` on `/bia/reports/print?...` with a print stylesheet; no new PDF dependency.
5. **`CampaignReport` (shared `campaigns.ts`)**: add
   - `returnedWithin30d: number | null`, `returnedWithin30dPct: number | null`, `returnWindowEndsAt: string`.
   - Definition: unique customers with at least one recorded visit at a participating shop in the campaign window who have another recorded visit at **any shop in the neighbourhood** during the 30 days after the campaign ends. Null until `returnWindowEndsAt` has passed; the UI shows "Available {date}". Baseline: same measure for the same-length window before the campaign, so the number has context. Aggregates only; document the definition in `campaigns.md` and in the report footer.
   - This is the first measure of the province study's unmeasured "conversion rate" indicator; keep the name "came back within 30 days" in the UI, and map it to the indicator on `/for-bias` only.
6. **Public leads** (`repApi.publicLeadBody`): add `kind: "restaurant"`, `language?: "en"|"hi"|"ur"|"pa"`, `role?`, `eventDate?`, `boardMeetingAt?`, `memberCount?`. Rep inbox shows language and board date; a BIA lead with a board date under 30 days is flagged.
7. **Sample report**: `SAMPLE_BOARD_REPORT` in `packages/shared` (typed as `CampaignReport` plus `sample: true`), used by `/for-bias` and the Figma numbers. The UI must render the "Sample data" tag whenever `sample` is true.
