# Design v3.2: Clear theme (glass revision)

Owner: Design. Status: **proposal for founder review, 2026-10-28, revision 2**. Nothing in this change touches app code. It is a second theme over the same structure as v3.1 Main Street Warm: same shell, same Today cards, same pages, same copy.

**History.** Revision 1 (flat neutral, white cards, Inter) was shown on 2026-10-28. Founder feedback: "This looks too bland have a glassy ui feel to it. Better fitting font so it fits the vibe as well with clean nice components and such." This page is revision 2. Revision 1 is kept in Figma on the page "v3.2 Clear theme" and in `scratchpad/clear/*.png`; this document replaces its token values.

**Figma:** [Local Loyalty v3 design system](https://www.figma.com/design/tat1lWRQIJ2Q3Al98pWOtA), page **v3.3 Clear glass** ([page link](https://www.figma.com/design/tat1lWRQIJ2Q3Al98pWOtA?node-id=45-2)). Frames: café Today 1440 light (`47:2`), café Today 1440 dark (`47:328`), café Today 390 (`47:654`), customers with glass detail sheet 1440 (`48:2`), counter tablet 1180 (`48:465`), customer pass 390 (`48:598`). The page has a `Clear Glass / Color` variable collection (29 variables, Light and Dark modes, with alpha); the dark Today frame is the light frame in the Dark mode with a dark backdrop. Frames use real background blur, inner top highlights and layered shadows; text is editable Plus Jakarta Sans and icons are Lucide vectors. Two nodes (the customer sheet's hairline stroke and the pass card's hairline stroke) use a literal 9% ink stroke because bound alpha variables rendered opaque on those two nodes; everything else is bound.

**HTML mock and PNGs (scratch, not committed):** `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-set-it-up-ai/5a1e2619-40bd-5f60-81fb-50d449557f59/scratchpad/clear/v2/` holds `mock.html` (open with `?s=today|today-m|customers|counter|pass|fonts`, `&t=dark`, `&l=ur`, `&f=geist`, `&solid=1`), `contrast.js` and `cc.py` (the AA check), the PNGs, `patterns-glass.md` and the reference captures.

## 1. The direction

Revision 1 removed colour and was read as bland. Revision 2 keeps the same rule (colour only for meaning) and adds depth instead of colour: frosted glass over a soft backdrop.

- **Backdrop.** A calm cool mesh behind everything: a pale base (`#EEF1F7` light, `#080A10` dark) with five large, very soft radial glows: indigo at the top left, a lighter blue at the top right, lilac in the middle, an aqua at the bottom left, and **the trade colour as a faint glow at the bottom right** (café brown at 28% in light, 16% in dark). It is fixed to the viewport so panels slide over it.
- **Glass panels.** Sidebar, top bar, cards, the customer sheet, the phone tab bar and the pass card. Each is a translucent white (light) or translucent slate (dark) fill with `backdrop-filter: blur(22px) saturate(1.5)`, a 1px white inner highlight on the top edge, a 1px ink hairline at 7% outside it, and two layered soft shadows. Radii are larger: cards 18, sidebar 22, sheet 24, pass 26, controls 11 to 14.
- **Soft inner pieces.** Icon chips, search field, quick action tiles and outline buttons are a lighter translucent fill with a white edge and **no blur of their own** (blur is expensive; see section 6).
- **One accent.** Indigo `#2F5BEA` (dark `#7391FF`) for the single primary action, links, the selected row, sparklines and "reward ready". The primary button is a soft-glow button: a top-lit indigo gradient, a 1px inner highlight and a coloured glow shadow.
- **Colour means status.** Green for "can text", "live" and increases. Amber and red are reserved for warnings and destructive actions. Counts such as "3 need you" are neutral pills.
- **Trade colour is a mark.** The 8px dot with a soft ring after the shop name, the one highlighted bar in "Visits by hour" (a light-to-trade gradient with a soft glow), the dot on the next stamp, and the backdrop glow. On the pass the whole backdrop is trade-tinted and the stamp discs carry a trade-coloured shadow and icon.
- **Plus Jakarta Sans.** See section 3.

Same structure, different surface: the sidebar items, top bar contents, every label and number on Today, the "Needs you" rows, the page order, the counter flow and the pass content are unchanged. Layout changes that are adaptation, not new information: the sidebar and top bar float inset 12px from the edges (the grid is the same), the four stats are four separate glass cards again (as in Warm, not the flat strip), "Visits by hour" gets a Today / Week segmented control where Warm had the word "Today", and the customer filters become one pill segmented control.

### 2.7 Contrast on glass (WCAG 2.x, measured)

Method: each screen was rendered, then rendered again with all text and icons hidden; for every text node the pixels under it (the real composite of glass over backdrop) were sampled and the darkest and lightest sample was tested against the text colour. Script: `v2/contrast.js` and `v2/cc.py`. Normal text needs 4.5:1; text at 24px or larger (or 18.66px bold) needs 3:1.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Today 1440 light | 71 | 4.93 ("Open counter", white on the primary gradient) | 0 |
| Today 1440 dark | 71 | 6.38 | 0 |
| Today 390 light | 50 | 4.93 | 0 |
| Today 390 dark | 50 | 6.28 | 0 |
| Customers with sheet, light (sheet text only) | 60 | 4.93 | 0 |
| Customers with sheet, dark | 60 | 6.83 | 0 |
| Counter light | 36 | 4.93 ("Find customer"); the 32px "/ 10" numeral is 3.80, large text | 0 |

Two fixes came out of the first run and are in the values above: group labels (11.5px) moved from `--subtle-foreground` (3.4 on glass) to `--muted-foreground`, and the primary gradient start moved from `#5379F6` (3.86 with white) to `#4668EE` (4.69). The check is a snapshot of the mock's backdrop. A future screen that puts text on a part of the backdrop that is darker or more saturated than these (for example directly over the indigo glow in a corner) should be re-run through `contrast.js`.

## 3. Type

**Plus Jakarta Sans for everything**, through `next/font/google` (`Plus_Jakarta_Sans`, `variable: "--font-jakarta"`, subsets `latin` and `latin-ext`, weights 400, 500, 600, 700; SIL Open Font License, free). Fallback stack is unchanged in order: the Noto script fonts for Devanagari, Gurmukhi, Nastaliq and Naskh (`SCRIPT_FONTS` in `css.ts`) follow it, then system fonts, so Hindi, Punjabi and Urdu stay Noto exactly as before.

**How it was chosen.** Candidates were limited to free faces that ship through `next/font/google`. Four were tested in a browser for tabular figures (the width of `1111111` and `0000000` with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`): **Geist, Plus Jakarta Sans, Manrope and Onest all have real tabular figures** (equal widths with `tnum`, different without). Two were put side by side on the Today screen (`clear-font-compare.png`, labelled A and B with a column of numbers, the 7 / 10 numeral and an Urdu line):

- **A, Plus Jakarta Sans (chosen).** Round geometric forms with open apertures and a generous x-height. It reads as friendly and soft, which matches 18 to 26px radii and translucent panels, and it has more character than Inter at headline sizes while staying plain in 13px table cells. Its numerals are clear and tabular; the bold weight on the stat numbers has a pleasant, slightly wide shape.
- **B, Geist.** Clean and sharp, but it is a close cousin of Inter. It would answer "better fitting font" with a change most people would not notice, and it feels more developer-tool than neighbourhood shop.
- **Manrope and Onest** were tabular-checked and set aside: Manrope is close to Plus Jakarta in feel but tighter and more technical in the numerals; Onest is a neutral grotesque like Geist.
- **Satoshi and General Sans** were considered. Both come from Fontshare under the ITF Free Font License. I could not retrieve the licence text from here (the licence page returned only the site tagline), so I can not state its terms; they are also not on Google Fonts, so they would need `next/font/local` with self-hosted files. Excluded until someone reads the licence.

Plus Jakarta Sans has a narrow word space; set `word-spacing: .07em` on the body (and nowhere else). It is about 3% wider than Inter, so a few labels wrap earlier on 390; the mock was checked at 390 and 1440.

## 6. Practical limits

**Blur budget.** `backdrop-filter` is the costly part, so it is spent on few, large layers.

| Screen | Blurred layers |
| --- | --- |
| Today 1440 | 10 (sidebar, top bar, four stat cards, Needs you, Visits by hour, Up next, Quick actions) |
| Today 390 | 10 (top bar, tab bar, Needs you, four stat cards, Visits by hour, Up next, Quick actions) |
| Customers with sheet | 4 (sidebar, top bar, table card, sheet) |
| Pass | 3 (pass card, Nearby, Passport) |
| **Counter tablet** | **2** (header and bottom dock; everything between them is solid) |

Rules: no more than 12 blurred layers visible at once; none inside a scrolling list row; never nest a blurred element inside another blurred one (inner tiles use `--glass-soft` without blur); blur radius at most 30px; the counter app never goes above 2. The counter body (keypad, stamp card, buttons) is solid on purpose: those are the surfaces staff hit forty times an hour on cheap tablets.

**Solid fallback.** Glass roles collapse to opaque values and the backdrop becomes flat in any of these cases:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce) { :root[data-theme-style="clear"] { /* solid */ } }
@supports not ((backdrop-filter: blur(1px)) or (-webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(1px))) { :root[data-theme-style="clear"] { /* solid */ } }
:root[data-theme-style="clear"].solid { /* solid: the same values, for a low-power switch */ }
```

"Solid" means: `--glass`, `--glass-strong`, `--glass-sheen` = `--glass-solid`; `--glass-soft` = `--muted`; `--glass-blur` = 0; `--backdrop` = `--background`; `--tile-*` = `--muted`; shadows reduced to one light layer. Layout, radii and every text colour stay the same, so contrast only improves. `clear-today-1440-solid-fallback.png` and `clear-today-dark-1440-solid-fallback.png` show it. A per-device low-power switch (a class `solid` on `<html>`, remembered locally) is recommended for counter tablets; the counter already limits itself to two layers.

**Contrast.** See 2.7. Everything measured passes AA in light, dark and Urdu.

**Dark mode.** Same roles, values above. The panels are translucent slate over a near-black base with a few percent of indigo and trade glow; the 1px white edge is 11% and the top highlight 8%. The primary is `#7391FF` with near-black text.

**Urdu RTL.** Verified in the mock at 390 and 1440 (`clear-today-ur-390.png`, `clear-today-ur-1440.png`). Rules: logical properties only (`padding-inline`, `inset-inline-end`, `border-inline-start`, no `left` or `right` in themed CSS), so the floating sidebar goes to the right, the sheet slides from the left, the selected-row accent bar moves to the inline start, the segmented control and tab bar mirror; directional icons flip; sparklines and the hour chart mirror with the page; Latin names and number runs inside Urdu lines sit in `<bdi>`; Nastaliq body line height 1.95 with no fixed row heights; Plus Jakarta Sans is first in the stack so digits and Latin letters stay in it, and Urdu falls to Noto Nastaliq Urdu. Urdu body reads small at 14px; use 15px body and 13.5px meta when `lang="ur"`. Urdu strings new to Clear need a native review. The Urdu render covers Today only.

## 7. Switching themes in the build

Add a `data-theme-style` attribute on `<html>` (absent means Warm; `"clear"` means Clear). Light and dark stay on `data-theme`. In `packages/design-tokens` (build team, not part of this change):

1. New `src/clear.ts` beside `warm.ts` with the values in section 2 and a `clearTradeColors(family, theme)` returning `{ accent, foreground, alt: accent, soft, deep }`.
2. In `css.ts` emit the third block set under `:root[data-theme-style="clear"]` for light and the two dark selectors, the 14 `[data-trade]` rules scoped the same way, the three fallback blocks from section 6, and the backdrop and glass roles. The attribute selector sits in front, so it beats Warm without `!important`.
3. Fonts: `:root[data-theme-style="clear"] { --font-sans: var(--font-jakarta), ...SCRIPT_FONTS...; --font-display: var(--font-sans); --font-wordmark: var(--font-sans); }` and load `Plus_Jakarta_Sans` in the root layout next to Inter. `body { word-spacing: .07em }` under the same selector.
4. Utility: a `.glass` (or `glass:` Tailwind variant) that applies `background: var(--glass-sheen, var(--glass)); backdrop-filter: blur(var(--glass-blur)) saturate(var(--glass-sat)); border: 1px solid var(--glass-border); box-shadow: var(--glass-inner), 0 0 0 1px var(--glass-edge), var(--glass-shadow)`. `.glass-soft` is the same without blur. Apply to the shell panels and cards, not to rows.
5. Radii and shadows: `--ll-radius-card: 18px`, `--ll-radius-tile: 11px`, `--radius: .6875rem` (11px); card shadow tokens point at `--glass-shadow`.
6. Structural changes (four stat cards with sparklines, floating sidebar and top bar, segmented controls, sheet, floating phone tab bar, backdrop on `body`) hang off `[data-theme-style="clear"]`.
7. Decide where the theme and the low-power switch are stored (per shop, per user, per device).

## 8. Open choices

1. **Chart highlight in the trade colour.** Keeps a piece of each trade's identity (and the backdrop glow carries more); the cost is a brown bar next to indigo sparklines for a café. Switching the highlight to `--foreground` is a one-line change.
2. **Backdrop intensity.** The mesh is deliberately quiet. A stronger one (indigo 52% to 65%) feels more "glass" but starts to compete with the content; the contrast check would need a re-run.
3. **Counter has glass only on its edges.** A fully glass counter looks richer but costs performance on the tablets and lowers legibility in a bright shop. Recommended as is.
4. **Plus Jakarta Sans vs Geist.** Chosen: Plus Jakarta Sans. If the team prefers a sharper look, Geist is a drop-in (`&f=geist` in the mock) and was also tabular-checked.
5. **Who picks the theme.** Per shop (the owner sets it for all staff) or per user.
