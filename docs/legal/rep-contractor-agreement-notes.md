# Onboarding reps: what to ask the employment lawyer

## Background for the lawyer

- Local Loyalty signs independent Toronto merchants onto a free loyalty network, one BIA strip at a time.
- Reps are mostly **students**. On a strip launch they work in blitzes: 2–4 reps for 6–8 weeks, then part-time follow-up.
- Each merchant takes about 2.5 paid hours: about 20 minutes on site plus prospecting, travel, and day-7 and day-30 calls.

**Original plan (now dropped):** commission-only student interns. Our understanding is that this is not lawful under the ESA for most interns. Unpaid placements are lawful only inside qualifying school programs ([McMillan](https://mcmillan.ca/insights/internal-affairs-managing-unpaid-internships-in-ontario/)).

**Current plan:** hourly employees at **$17.95/hr** (Ontario minimum wage from Oct 1, 2026; [Hicks Morley](https://hicksmorley.com/2026/04/01/ontario-minimum-wage-to-increase-october-1-2026/)), plus a **$25 activation bonus** per merchant that reaches 25 active members within 30 days. The model budgets 13% payroll burden on top: vacation pay, CPP, EI and WSIB.

## Questions to ask

### A. Classification

1. Should reps be employees, or can any be **independent contractors**?
   - Which factors matter most under the ESA and CRA tests in our facts: control of schedule, our scripts and tools, exclusivity, chance of profit, own equipment?
   - Our default assumption: **employees**.
2. If a rep runs their own sales agency, with other clients and their own staff, can we contract with the agency? What must the contract say, and what records should we keep?
3. Does Ontario's *Digital Platform Workers' Rights Act, 2022* apply if reps take assignments through our rep console? Our understanding is that it covers ride-share, delivery and courier work, so probably not.
4. How do co-op placements through accredited college or university programs work (the ESA exemption for secondary or post-secondary programs)? What must the placement agreement include? Can co-op students also earn the bonus?

### B. Pay structure

5. Is **hourly plus a per-activation bonus** compliant? Confirm that:
   - the bonus counts as wages for vacation pay (4%) and public holiday pay calculations;
   - a bonus can be conditioned on the merchant activating (25 active members in 30 days), and paid in the next pay period after it is earned;
   - what happens to an earned but unpaid bonus when a rep leaves.
6. Which hours must be paid?
   - travel between merchants;
   - travel from home to the first visit;
   - training;
   - waiting at a shop for the owner;
   - phone check-ins from home.
7. Does the **3-hour rule** apply to short shifts? Confirm eating periods (30 minutes after 5 hours) and overtime (after 44 hours per week).
8. Is there any minimum-wage category or exemption for **students** that applies to our roles? **VERIFY** current categories.
9. Should we reimburse expenses (TTC, mileage, phone data) or pay an allowance? What are the tax implications?

### C. Paperwork and compliance

10. What must we provide before the first shift?
    - the ESA poster;
    - a written employment agreement;
    - any written job information required under recent *Working for Workers* amendments, which depend on employer size.
11. Payroll setup: CRA payroll account, CPP, EI, ROEs, T4s. Is **WSIB** registration mandatory for our classification, or optional?
12. Which policies are required at our size?
    - workplace violence and harassment (OHSA);
    - an electronic monitoring policy (threshold 25+ employees: do we use location tracking in the rep console?);
    - a right-to-disconnect policy (threshold 25+).
13. Fixed-term contracts for blitz periods: termination notice and pay obligations at the end of the term, and when they become indefinite.
14. **Lone-worker safety** for in-store and evening visits: what training or check-in protocol should we document?
15. Is a working-alone or driving policy needed if reps use their own cars?

### D. Contract terms

16. What should the employment agreement include?
    - **termination clauses** that are enforceable, given recent Ontario case law voiding many "for cause" or ESA-minimum clauses;
    - confidentiality;
    - IP assignment for scripts and templates;
    - **no non-competes**: Ontario bans most non-competes for employees, so confirm non-solicit options instead.
17. Conduct rules for merchant visits:
    - no promises beyond the Merchant Agreement;
    - CASL training (no adding customers without consent);
    - restricted categories (pharmacy no-earn setup, no cannabis or vape shops).
18. Can bonuses be clawed back if a merchant activation turns out to be fraudulent, such as fake members? How do we draft that lawfully, without deductions from wages that the ESA prohibits?

### E. Other

19. **Canada Summer Jobs and co-op wage subsidies**: any employment-law conditions we must meet?
20. Does using **referral payments to merchants** (for example $25 credit for referring another shop) raise any issue? It is not employment, but does it raise tax or competition issues?
21. Founder and co-founder: are any obligations triggered if the founder takes no salary? Are there SR&ED implications (salary versus dividends)?

## Decisions to bring back from the call

| Decision | Default until counsel says otherwise |
| --- | --- |
| Rep status | Part-time hourly employees |
| Pay | $17.95/hr (indexed each Oct 1) + $25 activation bonus |
| Expenses | TTC reimbursed at cost; personal-vehicle use not allowed |
| Co-op | Only via accredited programs, with a placement agreement |
| Contractors | None, until a written opinion is obtained |
| Documents | Offer letter + employment agreement, handbook (harassment, safety, CASL, restricted categories), bonus policy |
